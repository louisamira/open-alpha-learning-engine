# Curriculum Scope

This document defines the first comprehensive targets for Algebra 1 and Accounting. The goal is not to recreate semester pacing. The goal is a compact mastery path that a highly intelligent, focused learner can complete in 4 to 6 hours per course while still producing real evidence of understanding.

## Design Standard

Each course should include:

- A validated concept graph
- Track, unit, and major concept orientation essays
- Diagnostic probes that skip what the learner already knows
- Deterministic practice wherever possible
- Misconception-specific remediation
- Mastery checks with evidence, not passive completion
- A final integrative task that proves transfer

Time budgets assume a strong learner, high focus, and adaptive skipping. A normal learner can take longer without changing the graph.

## Algebra 1 Accelerated Mastery

Target duration: 4 to 6 hours

Audience: a strong learner who needs a complete Algebra 1 spine, either as first exposure with high aptitude or as a rigorous refresh.

Mastery target: the learner can translate relationships into symbols, manipulate expressions and equations reliably, interpret linear and quadratic behavior, and explain each step using properties rather than memorized moves.

### Unit 1: Algebra As A Language Of Unknowns

Budget: 25 to 35 minutes

Concepts:

- Variables as unknowns and changing quantities
- Terms, coefficients, constants, expressions
- Equality as a relationship, not an answer box
- Number properties used in algebraic manipulation

Evidence:

- Identify parts of expressions
- Explain why `3x + 2x = 5x` but `3x + 2y` cannot simplify

### Unit 2: Simplifying Expressions

Budget: 35 to 50 minutes

Concepts:

- Combining like terms
- Distributive property
- Nested parentheses
- Negative signs and subtraction as adding the opposite

Evidence:

- Simplify linear expressions deterministically
- Detect partial distribution and sign mistakes

### Unit 3: Solving Equations And Inequalities

Budget: 50 to 70 minutes

Concepts:

- One-step and two-step equations
- Multi-step equations with variables on both sides
- Equations with no solution or infinitely many solutions
- Inequalities and graphing solution sets
- Compound inequalities

Evidence:

- Solve and check equations
- Preserve equality or inequality through each transformation
- Explain why multiplying an inequality by a negative reverses the sign

### Unit 4: Linear Functions

Budget: 55 to 75 minutes

Concepts:

- Coordinate plane
- Slope as rate of change
- Intercepts
- Slope-intercept, point-slope, and standard form
- Graphing linear equations
- Writing equations from tables, graphs, and word situations

Evidence:

- Move between table, graph, equation, and story
- Interpret slope and intercepts in context

### Unit 5: Systems Of Linear Equations

Budget: 35 to 50 minutes

Concepts:

- Intersection as simultaneous truth
- Solving by graphing
- Solving by substitution
- Solving by elimination
- No-solution and infinite-solution systems

Evidence:

- Solve systems and interpret the solution in context
- Choose a reasonable method for a given system

### Unit 6: Exponents, Polynomials, And Factoring

Budget: 55 to 75 minutes

Concepts:

- Exponent rules
- Scientific notation
- Polynomial vocabulary
- Adding and multiplying polynomials
- Greatest common factor
- Factoring trinomials
- Difference of squares

Evidence:

- Apply exponent rules without collapsing unlike structures
- Factor and expand to verify equivalence

### Unit 7: Quadratics

Budget: 45 to 65 minutes

Concepts:

- Quadratic patterns and parabolas
- Vertex, axis of symmetry, intercepts
- Solving by factoring
- Completing the square as structure
- Quadratic formula as a general tool

Evidence:

- Solve quadratic equations using the best available method
- Interpret roots and vertex in context

### Unit 8: Data, Modeling, And Transfer

Budget: 25 to 40 minutes

Concepts:

- Scatter plots
- Trend lines
- Linear vs nonlinear patterns
- Residual intuition
- Choosing a model

Evidence:

- Fit a simple linear model to a situation
- Explain where the model is useful and where it breaks

### Final Algebra Task

The learner receives a small real-world model, such as subscription pricing, simple projectile height, or savings growth. They must define variables, write expressions or equations, solve, graph if useful, and explain limitations.

## Accounting Course Sequence

Accounting should split into three accelerated courses. A college senior does not need CPA-level coverage by default, but should be able to understand how accounting creates trust, maintain basic books, read statements, catch common distortions, and ask competent questions.

## Accounting 101: Records, Trust, And Double Entry

Target duration: 4 to 6 hours

Mastery target: the learner can record ordinary transactions, explain the accounting equation, build a trial balance, and understand how journals become financial statements.

### Unit 1: What Accounting Is For

Budget: 25 to 35 minutes

Concepts:

- Accounting as shared evidence
- Entity assumption
- Accounting equation
- Assets, liabilities, equity, revenue, expenses
- Cash vs economic activity

Evidence:

- Classify accounts and explain how a transaction affects the equation

### Unit 2: Debits And Credits

Budget: 45 to 65 minutes

Concepts:

- Debit and credit as left and right, not good and bad
- Normal balances
- Double-entry logic
- T-accounts

Evidence:

- Predict whether an account increases by debit or credit
- Create balanced two-line entries

### Unit 3: Journal Entries

Budget: 65 to 85 minutes

Concepts:

- Cash sales
- Credit sales
- Owner investment
- Borrowing
- Buying supplies and equipment
- Paying expenses
- Paying down liabilities

Evidence:

- Record deterministic journal entries from plain-language transactions
- Identify balanced but wrong entries

### Unit 4: Ledger And Trial Balance

Budget: 45 to 60 minutes

Concepts:

- Posting from journal to ledger
- Running account balances
- Trial balance
- Error detection limits

Evidence:

- Post a small transaction set and produce a trial balance

### Unit 5: First Financial Statements

Budget: 55 to 75 minutes

Concepts:

- Income statement
- Statement of owner equity
- Balance sheet
- How net income flows into equity
- Why profit is not cash

Evidence:

- Build simple statements from a trial balance

### Final Accounting 101 Task

The learner keeps books for a tiny business for one month: owner investment, purchases, revenue, expenses, borrowing, and payment. They produce journal entries, ledger balances, trial balance, income statement, and balance sheet.

## Accounting 201: Accrual Accounting And Operating Reality

Target duration: 4 to 6 hours

Mastery target: the learner can explain and apply accrual accounting, adjusting entries, inventory basics, receivables, payables, depreciation, and cash flow classification.

### Unit 1: Accrual vs Cash Basis

Budget: 40 to 55 minutes

Concepts:

- Revenue recognition
- Matching principle
- Receivables and payables
- Unearned revenue
- Prepaid expenses

Evidence:

- Decide when revenue and expense should be recognized

### Unit 2: Adjusting Entries

Budget: 65 to 85 minutes

Concepts:

- Accrued revenue
- Accrued expense
- Deferred revenue
- Prepaid expenses
- Depreciation
- Bad debt estimate

Evidence:

- Record period-end adjustments and explain the before/after effect

### Unit 3: Inventory And Cost Of Goods Sold

Budget: 45 to 65 minutes

Concepts:

- Inventory as asset
- Cost of goods sold
- Gross margin
- FIFO, LIFO, weighted average at intuition level
- Shrinkage and write-downs

Evidence:

- Compute simple gross profit and explain inventory flow assumptions

### Unit 4: Cash Flow Statement

Budget: 55 to 75 minutes

Concepts:

- Operating, investing, and financing activities
- Indirect method intuition
- Net income vs cash from operations
- Working capital effects

Evidence:

- Classify cash flows and explain why profitable companies can run short of cash

### Unit 5: Internal Controls And Closing

Budget: 35 to 50 minutes

Concepts:

- Separation of duties
- Reconciliations
- Closing temporary accounts
- Audit trail
- Materiality

Evidence:

- Identify weak controls in a small-business scenario

### Final Accounting 201 Task

The learner receives messy monthly activity and must make adjusting entries, update statements, classify cash flows, and explain what changed between cash profit and accrual profit.

## Accounting 301: Financial Analysis, Quality, And Decision Use

Target duration: 4 to 6 hours

Mastery target: the learner can read financial statements critically, analyze business performance, understand common distortions, and connect accounting numbers to operating decisions.

### Unit 1: Statement Analysis

Budget: 50 to 70 minutes

Concepts:

- Common-size statements
- Trend analysis
- Gross, operating, and net margins
- Liquidity ratios
- Leverage ratios
- Return metrics

Evidence:

- Interpret ratios without treating them as magic scores

### Unit 2: Revenue, Expenses, And Earnings Quality

Budget: 50 to 70 minutes

Concepts:

- Aggressive revenue recognition
- Capitalizing vs expensing
- One-time items
- Reserves and estimates
- Channel stuffing and timing games

Evidence:

- Spot warning signs in a narrative and financial excerpt

### Unit 3: Unit Economics And Managerial Accounting

Budget: 55 to 75 minutes

Concepts:

- Fixed vs variable costs
- Contribution margin
- Break-even
- Pricing decisions
- Make-or-buy intuition
- Relevant costs

Evidence:

- Compute break-even and recommend an action with assumptions stated

### Unit 4: Budgeting And Forecasting

Budget: 45 to 65 minutes

Concepts:

- Operating budget
- Cash budget
- Scenario planning
- Sensitivity analysis
- Forecast drivers

Evidence:

- Build a simple driver-based forecast and identify the sensitive assumptions

### Unit 5: Taxes, Equity, And Advanced Boundaries

Budget: 35 to 55 minutes

Concepts:

- Tax vs book income at a high level
- Stock-based compensation intuition
- Deferred revenue in software businesses
- Leases and debt commitments
- What to escalate to a CPA

Evidence:

- Explain what the learner can responsibly analyze and what requires specialist review

### Final Accounting 301 Task

The learner reviews a simplified company packet: income statement, balance sheet, cash flow, notes, and operating metrics. They must identify business health, accounting quality risks, cash risks, and the next five questions a competent operator or investor should ask.

## Build Priority

1. Finish the Algebra 1 graph through equations and linear functions before adding a web lesson renderer.
2. Finish Accounting 101 as the first practical adult-learning course.
3. Add Accounting 201 after journal entries, trial balance, and first statements are deterministic.
4. Add Accounting 301 once statement rendering and scenario evaluation exist.
5. Keep Chemistry at the seed vertical slice until Algebra 1 and Accounting have complete graph coverage.
